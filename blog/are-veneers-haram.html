Are Veneers Haram? What Happens to Your Teeth — Honest Guide

By Dr. Abdullah Karamat, FCPS Prosthodontist, Certified Implantologist
Last updated: September 2026
In short: Two questions come up more than almost any other before someone commits to veneers — is this permissible, and what actually happens to the tooth underneath. On the first: this is a personal question of faith, and we're not qualified to issue a religious ruling — we'd encourage you to raise it with your own religious guidance, and we're glad to explain the clinical facts so that conversation is an informed one. On the second: porcelain veneers involve removing a thin layer of enamel and are permanent; composite veneers are far more conservative and largely reversible. Both typically last 8–10 years before needing replacement — not forever, whatever else you've read.

Is getting veneers haram?

We won't tell you what's permissible in your faith — that's not our place, and we'd genuinely encourage you to raise it with your own religious guidance rather than take a dentist's word for it.

What we can share is how we think about it clinically. We don't believe veneers should be a purely elective, want-it-because-it's-trendy treatment. We treat based on need — where there's a genuine indication (a chipped, worn, badly discoloured, or damaged tooth), the case for treatment is straightforward. Many people find that distinction — necessity versus purely cosmetic change — matters to how they think through the permissibility question too, but that's a conversation for you and your own religious guidance, not for us.

What we can tell you plainly is the clinical side of "permanent" — which is the next section, because it's usually part of the same question.

What actually happens to your natural teeth

This is where porcelain and composite genuinely diverge, and it's worth understanding before you decide between them.

Porcelain (E.max) veneers require removing a thin layer of enamel from the front of the tooth — typically less than half a millimetre, but enamel doesn't grow back. Once it's done, that tooth will need some kind of covering (a veneer, or eventually a crown) for the rest of its life. This is the honest tradeoff for the strongest, most stain-resistant, longest-lasting result. We recommend E.max over zirconia veneers specifically because E.max gives a far more natural, lifelike appearance and can be properly bonded to the tooth — zirconia looks noticeably less natural and can't be reliably bonded without a zirconia primer, which isn't available in Pakistan, and even where it is, few practices use it because of the added cost. It's part of why we don't offer zirconia veneers at all.

Composite veneers are bonded directly onto the tooth surface with little to no enamel removal in most cases. They're more conservative, more affordable (PKR 12,000/tooth vs PKR 35,000/tooth for E.max), and can often be adjusted or removed later without permanently altering the tooth underneath. The tradeoff: they don't resist staining or wear as well, and typically need replacing sooner.

Composite is a good option if you want to "try out" a new smile before committing to porcelain, or if minimal-to-no tooth preparation genuinely matters to you — though the trade-off is worth being clear-eyed about: the frequent need to redo or replace composite veneers can offset that advantage over time.

How long do veneers actually last?

Both types wear out — this isn't a one-time, forever decision. E.max veneers typically last 8–10 years with normal care before they need replacing, and can last noticeably longer when the case is selected well in the first place and the patient looks after them properly. Composite veneers usually need touch-ups or replacement sooner, often within 3–5 years — and even when they survive without breaking, composite will eventually pick up staining that porcelain resists far better.

If you've seen claims elsewhere promising veneers that last "a lifetime" or are "everlastingly perfect" — that's not how any dental material actually behaves, porcelain included.

Everyday worries: can you actually live normally with veneers?

Yes, close to normally — but a few real, common questions:

Can you bite into an apple? You can, but it's worth being sensible about it — we'd advise not relying on your veneers for hard biting where you can avoid it, simply to protect your investment for as long as possible.

Do they feel different day to day? Yes, for a short while. Most patients notice the shape or feel is slightly different for the first few days to a couple of weeks, then stop thinking about it entirely once they're used to it.

Can you go back to your natural teeth later? For composite — largely yes. For porcelain — not fully, which is exactly why the enamel-removal point above matters before you commit.

Ready to talk it through?

Every case is different, and the right answer — porcelain, composite, or neither — usually comes down to a proper look at your teeth, not a blog post. If you're also wondering whether clear aligners might be a better first step for your situation, we can assess that at the same consultation — many patients benefit from straightening the teeth first before any cosmetic work. Frequently Asked Questions

Is it haram to shave down teeth for veneers?
This is a question for your own religious guidance, not for us to answer.What we can tell you clinically is that porcelain veneers do require removing a thin layer of enamel — typically under half a millimetre — and that's part of what makes the decision worth thinking through properly before committing, whatever your reasoning.
What happens to teeth under veneers after 20 years?
With proper bonding, the tooth underneath stays stable — it's the veneer that eventually needs replacing, not the tooth.With ceramic veneers placed using resin cement and a full bonding protocol, there's minimal to no leakage at the margins. Composite veneers are more prone to some leakage over time due to natural shrinkage in the material, which is one reason they don't last as long.
Is a veneer good for teeth, or does it damage them?
Done correctly, a veneer doesn't damage the tooth — but porcelain preparation is permanent, which is the real thing to weigh.A proper bonding protocol protects the tooth well for years. The honest caveat is with porcelain specifically: the enamel removal itself is permanent, so the real question isn't whether veneers damage teeth day to day, it's whether that permanent step is the right call for a particular tooth. That's exactly why we treat on a need basis rather than offering it as a purely cosmetic add-on.
Can Muslims get veneers for medical or dental reasons, like a broken or damaged tooth?
That's a question for your own religious guidance — but many people find a genuine dental need sits differently than a purely cosmetic change.A broken, badly worn, or damaged tooth is often viewed differently than an elective cosmetic change when people think through the permissibility question, but that distinction is for you to work through with your own religious guidance, not something we're positioned to rule on.
What happens if a ceramic veneer chips or breaks?
A small chip can sometimes be polished smooth; a real break usually means replacement, not repair.Most genuine breaks or cracks in porcelain can't be fixed the way a filling can — the veneer needs to be replaced. This is one reason we advise against using veneered teeth for hard biting (opening packets, biting nails, etc.).
Does the colour of veneers change over time?
Porcelain holds its colour for years; composite will eventually stain.Porcelain (E.max) veneers resist staining well and keep their shade long-term. Composite veneers are more porous and pick up staining from tea, coffee, or smoking over time — one reason they need replacing sooner. Worth knowing upfront: porcelain can't be whitened later, so shade gets chosen carefully before placement, not adjusted after.
Can you still get a cavity on a tooth with a veneer?
Yes — a veneer covers the front surface, not the whole tooth.Decay can still develop at the edges or on untreated surfaces if oral hygiene isn't kept up. Regular brushing, flossing, and check-ups matter just as much with veneers as without them.
Do you need a night guard if you have veneers?
If you grind or clench at night, yes.Grinding is one of the more common reasons a veneer chips or fails early, and a properly fitted night guard is a small, cheap way to protect a much bigger investment.
