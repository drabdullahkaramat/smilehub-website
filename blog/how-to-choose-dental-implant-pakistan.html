How to Choose a Dental Implant in Pakistan

Dr. Abdullah Karamat
BDS, FCPS · Prosthodontist · Certified Implantologist
July 2026 · 8 min read

Dental implants in Pakistan are a crowded, loudly-marketed market — “Swiss implants,” “permanent teeth in 3 days,” “no bone graft needed.” Some of it is real dentistry. Some of it is salesmanship wearing a white coat. As a prosthodontist who places and restores implants — and who regularly treats the aftermath of implant decisions gone wrong — this is the guide I wish every patient read before paying anyone, including us.

The Most Important Truth: The Operator Matters More Than the Brand

Patients ask “which implant brand is best?” — but the evidence and our daily experience agree: a well-planned, well-placed implant from a reputable mid-range system will outperform a premium implant placed poorly. Planning on a CBCT scan, correct positioning, clean surgical protocol, and a properly designed crown determine success far more than the logo on the box. So the first question is never “which implant?” — it is “who is planning, placing, and restoring it, and what is their training?”

What a Legitimate Implant System Looks Like

Brand still matters — just not the way the ads suggest. A legitimate system has: published documentation and long-term studies; international presence, so parts exist outside one importer’s warehouse; component availability for decades, because your implant will need service — a crown replacement, a screw, an abutment — long after placement; and a clinic willing to name it before treatment and document it afterwards. At Smile Hub we place BioHorizons (USA) and Izen (Korea), tell you exactly which and why, and hand over an implant passport — the brand, model, size, and batch of what went into your jaw — so any dentist, anywhere, can service it for the rest of your life. A clinic that cannot or will not do this is telling you something.

Basal Implants: Our Honest Position

You will encounter clinics — increasingly, general dentists after short courses — marketing basal (cortical / “strategic”) implants with seductive promises: no bone graft ever, permanent teeth in 3 days, suitable for everyone. Our position is blunt: we never place basal implants, and we consider the way they are being sold in Pakistan a disservice to patients.

Why. Mainstream implantology worldwide has largely moved away from basal designs: the long-term evidence base is weak, the technique bypasses rather than solves poor bone (the graft you were promised to skip was there to protect your future), and — the part the ads never mention — when basal implants fail, they tend to fail catastrophically: extensive bone destruction around long cortically-anchored metal, making retrieval traumatic and re-treatment far harder than if conventional planning had been done first. We see these patients after the fact. The revision is always harder than the right treatment would have been.

If a clinic’s central pitch is speed and graft-avoidance rather than planning and evidence, take that as your signal to get a second opinion — from anyone, not necessarily us.

“Swiss Implants”: How to Verify Origin Claims Yourself

Implant marketing in Pakistan leans heavily on European origin — and here is the honest distinction every patient should know: “Swiss technology,” “Swiss design,” and “German engineering” are not the same claim as “manufactured in Switzerland.” Genuine Swiss-made systems exist — and so does a lot of borrowed geography. We are not going to name names; we are going to hand you the verification method, which is better:

1. Ask for the exact brand and model name — in writing, before treatment. 2. Look up the manufacturer’s own website — not the importer’s — and find where it is actually manufactured. 3. Ask the clinic directly: “Where is this implant manufactured?” and watch whether the answer is a place or a slogan. 4. Ask whether you will receive an implant passport with the batch sticker — unverifiable systems usually cannot produce one. A clinic with nothing to hide answers all four in thirty seconds. We do — and any good clinic will.

Six Questions to Ask Any Dentist Before Saying Yes

1. Who places the implant, who restores it, and what is their specialist training? 2. Which exact implant system — and where is it manufactured? 3. Will I receive an implant passport? 4. Is my plan based on a CBCT scan? 5. What is the total staged cost — implant, abutment, crown — in writing? 6. What happens if it fails — is there a warranty? Ask us these questions too. Clinics that welcome them are the ones you want; clinics that deflect them have answered you already.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are basal implants good?
We never place them, and we advise strong caution. They are marketed with promises like “no bone graft” and “teeth in 3 days,” but mainstream implantology has largely moved away from them: the long-term evidence is weak, and in our experience, when they fail they fail catastrophically — with bone destruction that makes rescue far harder. Conventional, properly planned implants remain the evidence-based standard.
How do I verify where an implant is actually made?
Ask for the exact brand and model, then check the manufacturer’s own website for where it is manufactured — not “designed.” “Swiss technology” is not “made in Switzerland.” A trustworthy clinic names the system before treatment and gives you an implant passport with the batch sticker afterwards. Hesitation to name the brand is itself an answer.
Which implant brand is best in Pakistan?
Honestly: the operator matters more than the brand. A well-placed mid-range implant outperforms a poorly placed premium one. What matters in a brand is documentation, international presence, and decades of parts availability. We place BioHorizons (USA) and Izen (Korea) and tell every patient exactly what is going into their jaw.
What questions should I ask before getting an implant?
Six: Who places and who restores it, and what is their training? Which exact system and where is it manufactured? Will I get an implant passport? Is the plan CBCT-based? What is the total staged cost in writing? And what happens if it fails — is there a warranty? Good clinics welcome all six.
What is an implant passport?
A document recording the exact brand, model, size, and batch of your implant. Implants need service over a lifetime, and any future dentist anywhere needs to know what system is in the bone to source parts. Clinics using unverifiable systems often cannot provide one — that absence tells you something.
Are “permanent teeth in 3 days” offers trustworthy?
Be careful. Legitimate immediate-loading protocols exist for selected cases with good bone — we offer them where indicated. But “teeth in 3 days for everyone, no graft ever” is a sales pitch, not a treatment plan — and it is the signature marketing of basal implant providers. Biology sets the timeline, not the advertisement.
